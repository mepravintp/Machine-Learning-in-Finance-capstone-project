What earns the highest marks

Model complexity is not rewarded, though experiments are welcome.

Ground rules in the brief

  • Make suitable assumptions where needed, and write them down.
  • State risks and limitations clearly: data limits (it is synthetic), model risk, and what could go wrong.
  • Model governance and explainability matter, so use SHAP and plain-English readings of the drivers.
  • Senior management wants actionable inputs, not model scores for their own sake.

Module section template

  1. Business question in one sentence.
  2. Data and cleaning in a few lines, with any leakage you removed.
  3. Method: models compared and why, split type, metrics.
  4. Result: one table or chart, plus the top SHAP drivers in plain words.
  5. Decision: the financial metric and the recommendation it supports.
  6. Risks and caveats: what would make you distrust the result.
  7. Row for the allocation table.

Governance checklist

  • Random seed fixed (42) and every notebook runs top to bottom.
  • Splits stated for each module (stratified, or time-based for time series).
  • Leakage checked: no target-derived features, scalers fit on training data only.
  • Imbalanced metrics reported (precision, recall, PR-AUC, confusion matrix).
  • SHAP drivers explained in business language.
  • Assumptions listed in one table with their sources.
  • Limitations listed for every module.
  • Numbers match across report, deck and notebooks.

Writing for senior management

  • Lead with the decision, then the evidence.
  • One chart or table per claim, with a title that states the finding.
  • Express results in rupees and risk, not only AUC or RMSE.
  • Say what you would do differently with real data.
  • Cut anything that does not change a decision.
